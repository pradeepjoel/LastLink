# Architecture and routing

The Next.js browser sends a simulated arrival scenario to FastAPI. SQLite holds separate validated snapshots of official Lignes d’Azur and ZOU Express GTFS. The importer stores stops and coordinates, routes, trips, stop times, calendars, exceptions, optional transfer rules, and source provenance. It writes a temporary database and replaces the previous snapshot only after validation.

The router builds a small time-dependent graph from **actual trips on active service dates**. A ride edge links boarding and alighting calls on the same trip, in stop order, respecting pickup/drop-off restrictions. Verified stop groups are Terminal 2, Grand Arénas, Jean Médecin, Nice Saint-Augustin, Antibes, and Cannes. The only cross-feed pedestrian edge is Grand Arénas → Nice Saint-Augustin, enabled after coordinate checks and charged an 8-minute assumed walk. Shared Terminal 2 stops in the two feeds are matched by nearby coordinates. No general proximity-based transfer is created.

A bounded time-dependent label-correcting search enumerates simple itineraries with up to four vehicle legs and prunes dominated partial journeys by arrival time and transfer-risk class. It applies the airport stop-access and boarding estimates, a transfer buffer, and GTFS transfer prohibitions/minimums when a rule matches the exact stop IDs. It searches to the next 04:00 local service-window boundary. UTC instants are used for comparisons, while GTFS service-day clocks and passenger display use Europe/Paris. Ambiguous or nonexistent passenger wall times require an explicit UTC offset.

The assessment independently evaluates the original no-delay plan and the delayed scenario. The first boarding must remain reachable after stop access and boarding time; the uncertainty band and tight transfer slack produce `AT_RISK`. Options from stale or out-of-validity feeds are `UNKNOWN`. The recommendation favors feasible, then at-risk complete journeys and considers arrival, transfers, and walking. The last connection is the complete feasible itinerary with the latest first departure, not simply the last vehicle leaving the airport.

This is a purpose-built corridor router with explicit scope. It does not model all station entrances, platform changes, accessibility, traffic, fares, realtime operations, or guaranteed boarding. No AI model, worker, Redis, or paid service participates in routing.
